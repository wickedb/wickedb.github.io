# Bungo Stray Dogs and morality

BSD is an anime/manga by Kafka Asagiri with all of its characters named after a real-life author (much like the Author's pseudonym Kafka), and their 'special-abilities' named after a seminal work by the author. The show follows a few 'teams', so far, a detective agency, a mafia, an english agency, a military agency, and a crime agency. This write-up is supposed to put forward the theory that morality, in BSD, is negotiable, as opposed to a rigid individual characteristic. This write up contains spoilers and assumes familiarity with the series.

The central negotitation is between Odasaku and Dazai - Oda's dying negotiation shifts Dazai's morality, causing him to join the detective agency abandoning his nothing-matters attitude. 
Dazai says, "I always end up losing what I hold closest. It is a given that everything worth wanting, will be lost the moment I obtain it. There is nothing worth pursuing at the cost of prolonging a life of suffering". After which Odasaku's dying negotiation is "nothing in this world can fill that lonely hole you have.... be on the side that saves people.. neither good nor evil means much to you, I know, but that'd make you at least a little bit better". 

Even if Dazai retains his self-destructive attempts after joining the detective agency, the promise to Odasaku forces him to apply himself to ensuring the goals of the agency is met. His behaviour as a mentor to Atsushi is vastly different from his treatment of Akutagawa back when he was in the mafia. In the book No Longer Human by Osamu Dazai, the protagonist characterizes himself as passive even though the narration is in first person. On some level, he doesn't see the point in trying. Kafka Asagiri retains this core feeling of pointlessness in his Dazai, but through interactions with other characters alchemizes it into something different. 

The show/manga even has a separate treatment of 'combinations' of powers, Ango (s2 ep3), "the govt has observed that when some abilities interact, they may careen off into an entirely different unexpected direction in rare cases". Chuuya and Dazai are together known as double black, Atsushi and Akutagawa are also able to fuse their abilities. The fusion of abilities is symbolic of two independent parts interacting, through a social contract of sorts, to become something of its own. 

Another recurring negotiation is between Fukuzawa and Mori - the heads of two rival agencies - the detective and mafia respectively. For example, who gets to have Yosano, the arrangement that the detective agency guards the day while the mafia would guard the night, and requesting help during the page-arc. In s2 ep9, Fukuzawa negotiates with Mori to team up against Fitzgerald: "As people of this city, as organizations that work to protect it, we cannot allow foreign ability uses to raze it". Fukuzawa's special ability itself - all men are equal - is a sort of social contract with the members of the agencies. The whole agency is built around Ranpo, a non-ability user, who Fukuzawa convinces to use his talent for people instead of spiraling into being anti-social. In s4 ep2, we see Ranpo realize: "all that suffering wasn't because there's something wrong with me....no one ever despised me". 

The show is filled with negotiations of morality - some violent, some friendly, some unintentional. Atsushi, the protagonist of the show, is constantly negotiating with characters. He negotiates with kyouka to get her to leave the mafia, he negotiates with lucy saying he understands her loneliness, he is constantly negotiating with akutagawa - even getting him to stop killing for 6 months, in the end he even negotiates with teruko to let him out of captivity. This is Atsushi asking for Lucy's co-operation in s2 ep8, "I know your fear and loneliness well. But loneliness wasn't a king lording over us forever. Loneliness is just a soft, puffy cloud forming and vanishing on a whim. Many people on the ground carry wounds the way we do. Abandon them, and you're abandoning your past self. Is that what you want?"

All human beings are social, amenable, gullible, influential, and influencible. Morality, instead of being an immutable individual property, is seen as the outcome of social negotiations or contracts. The BEAST light novel further drives this point home, because when Akutagawa swaps places with Atsushi, he has more negotiations available to him (for example, Kunikida and Kenji), and turns out a lot differently. Similarly with atsushi, now in the mafia and under Mori, has a darker relationship with his past. 

All of the villains (before Fyodor) have been able to listen at some point - Mori helps Fukuzawa against Fitzgerald, Fitzgerald helps the agency against the Angels of decay, Fukuchi can hold negotiations with Fukuzawa, all of the Hunting Dogs members are influenced in some way, Sigma is able to switch loyalties, Bram Stoker can sacrifice himself for a little girl, etc. Moreover, what makes Fyodor the ultimate villain, is his inflexibility to negotiation. 

The Mersault scenes where Dazai and Fyodor are cell neighbours can be seen as Dazai's attempt to negotiate with him. BSD doesn't characterize its villains by their kill-count, goodness, or strength. Villainy in BSD is to be unamenable to negotiation. To not even hear an appeal. If morality, instead of being an individual trait, is a communal responsibility - then we have no moral high grounds - only culpability - in the face of immorality.
